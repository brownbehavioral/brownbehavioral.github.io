THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION. PLEASE REVIEW IT CAREFULLY.

This Notice also describes how records of substance use disorder treatment are protected under federal law, what rights you have over your information, and how to file a complaint if you believe your privacy rights have been violated. You have a right to a paper or electronic copy of this Notice, and a right to discuss it with the contact person named at the end.

Who Follows This Notice

This Notice applies to Brown Behavioral Health Services, PLLC (“BBHS,” “we,” “us”) at every location and in every program we operate, including our Rural Health Clinic and primary care services, outpatient behavioral health and psychiatry, our office-based opioid treatment and medication assisted treatment program, counseling and therapy services, the DUI school, personal support services, school based and mobile services, remote patient monitoring, correctional health services, and telehealth. It applies to all of our employees, contractors, students, trainees, and volunteers, at both our Bolivar, Tennessee office and our Olive Branch, Mississippi office.

Our Pledge to You

We are required by law to protect the privacy of your health information, to give you this Notice of our legal duties and privacy practices, to follow the terms of the Notice that is currently in effect, and to notify you if a breach occurs that compromises the privacy or security of your information. We will not use or share your information other than as described here unless you tell us in writing that we may.

Special protection for substance use disorder records. If you receive substance use disorder treatment from us, including treatment in our medication assisted treatment program, those records carry an additional layer of federal protection under 42 C.F.R. Part 2 that is stronger than HIPAA alone. The section titled Substance use disorder treatment records on the following pages explains those protections and your rights under that law.

How We May Use and Share Your Health Information

For your treatment

We use and share your information to provide, coordinate, and manage your care. For example, your psychiatric nurse practitioner may review your therapist’s progress notes and your laboratory results before adjusting a medication, and we may send your records to a specialist we refer you to.

For payment

We use and share your information to bill and collect payment for the care we provide. For example, we may send a claim with your diagnosis and the services you received to TennCare, Medicare, or your health plan, or seek prior authorization before a medication is dispensed.

For health care operations

We use and share your information to run the clinic and improve the care we deliver. For example, we may review a sample of charts for quality assurance, use your information to train students and staff, or provide information to our accreditation, licensing, or auditing professionals.

Other ways we may use or share your information without your written permission

Federal and state law permit or require us to use or share your health information for the following purposes. In each case we share only what is permitted and, where the rule requires it, only the minimum amount necessary.

  • When required by law. When a federal, state, or local law requires the disclosure.
  • Public health activities. To prevent or control disease, injury, or disability; to report births, deaths, and reactions to medications; to report to the Tennessee or Mississippi immunization registry; and to notify a person who may have been exposed to a communicable disease.
  • Abuse, neglect, or domestic violence. To report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a child or an adult to the agency authorized to receive the report, as Tennessee and Mississippi law require.
  • Health oversight. To an agency conducting audits, investigations, inspections, or licensure actions, such as a state licensing board or a Medicaid program integrity unit.
  • Judicial and administrative proceedings. In response to a court or administrative order, or to a subpoena or discovery request where the required assurances about notice to you have been given. Substance use disorder records are subject to stricter rules, described below.
  • Law enforcement. In the limited circumstances the law allows, such as responding to a court order or warrant, identifying a suspect or missing person, or reporting a crime on our premises. Substance use disorder records are subject to stricter rules, described below.
  • Serious threat to health or safety. To prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat to you or to another person, consistent with our duty under Tennessee and Mississippi law.
  • Coroners, medical examiners, and funeral directors. To allow them to carry out their duties.
  • Organ and tissue donation. To organ procurement organizations.
  • Research. When an institutional review board or privacy board has approved the research and the required privacy protections are in place.
  • Workers’ compensation. As authorized by workers’ compensation laws.
  • Military, veterans, national security, and correctional institutions. In the specific situations the law allows, including information about an inmate when a correctional institution needs it for the inmate’s health or the safety of others.
  • Business associates. To companies that perform services for us, such as our electronic health record vendor, billing company, answering service, or shredding service. Each one signs a written agreement requiring it to protect your information, and any vendor that may receive substance use disorder records must also sign a Qualified Service Organization Agreement.

People involved in your care

Unless you object, we may share information with a family member, friend, or other person you identify as involved in your care or payment for your care, limited to what that person needs to know. In an emergency, or when you are not able to agree or object, we will use our professional judgment about what is in your best interest. Substance use disorder records may not be shared this way without your written consent.

Appointment reminders, treatment alternatives, and health benefits

We may contact you to remind you of an appointment, to tell you about treatment options, or to tell you about health related benefits or services that may interest you. If you want us to contact you only in a certain way or at a certain number or address, tell us and we will accommodate reasonable requests.

Artificial intelligence assistance with documentation

We may use approved artificial intelligence tools to help with documentation and administrative work, under written agreements that require the vendor to protect your information and prohibit the vendor from using our patients’ information to train its products. A licensed provider reviews and takes responsibility for every record an AI tool helps prepare. We will not record your visit without first obtaining your separate written or verbal consent. You may decline or withdraw your consent at any time, and your care, your medications, your appointments, and your participation in any program will not be affected in any way.

Uses and Disclosures That Require Your Written Permission

The following always require your written authorization, which you may revoke in writing at any time, except to the extent we have already acted in reliance on it.

  • Psychotherapy notes. These are the notes a therapist keeps separate from the rest of your record to document or analyze a counseling session. They receive special protection and are not released without your written authorization, except in the narrow circumstances the law allows.
  • Marketing. Most uses of your information to market a product or service to you.
  • Sale of your information. We do not and will not sell your health information.
  • Any other use or disclosure not described in this Notice.

Substance Use Disorder Treatment Records

Records of your substance use disorder treatment are protected by federal law at 42 U.S.C. § 290dd-2 and 42 C.F.R. Part 2, in addition to HIPAA. The following protections apply to those records.

  • Written consent is required. Generally we may not tell anyone outside BBHS that you attend our program, or release any information identifying you as having a substance use disorder, unless you consent in writing, a court order permits it, or a narrow exception applies, such as a medical emergency, qualified research, an audit or program evaluation, or a report of suspected child abuse or neglect.
  • One consent can cover future care. You may give a single written consent that permits us to use and disclose your substance use disorder records for all future treatment, payment, and health care operations, rather than signing a new consent each time.
  • You may revoke your consent. You may revoke a consent at any time, in writing or orally, under 42 C.F.R. §§ 2.31 and 2.35, except to the extent we have already acted in reliance on it.
  • Your records cannot be used against you. Your substance use disorder records, and any testimony about them, may not be used or disclosed in any civil, criminal, administrative, or legislative proceeding against you unless you give specific written consent for that purpose or a court issues an order that meets the requirements of 42 C.F.R. Part 2, Subpart E. Before such an order is issued you are entitled to notice and an opportunity to be heard where the law requires it, and the order must be accompanied by a subpoena or other legal mandate before we will release the records.
  • Limits on sharing again. A person or organization that receives your substance use disorder records from us with your consent for treatment, payment, or health care operations may share them again as HIPAA permits, but the protection against use in legal proceedings against you continues to apply to those records.
  • Reporting a crime. Federal law and regulations do not protect any information about a crime committed by a patient either at the program or against any person who works for the program, or about any threat to commit such a crime.

More Protective Laws

Where Tennessee law, Mississippi law, or another federal law gives your information more protection than HIPAA does, we follow the more protective law. This includes Tennessee law governing the confidentiality of mental health and developmental disability records, laws governing HIV and sexually transmitted infection information, and laws governing the health information of minors. In some situations a minor patient controls the release of his or her own records; where that is the case, we follow the law that applies to that service.

Your Rights

You have the following rights regarding the health information we keep about you. To exercise any of them, contact the Privacy Officer named at the end of this Notice. We will not retaliate against you for exercising any right described here.

  • Get a copy of your records. You may inspect and get a paper or electronic copy of the health information we use to make decisions about your care. We will act on your request within 30 days. We may charge a reasonable, cost based fee. In limited circumstances we may deny a request, and where the law provides it you may have that denial reviewed.
  • Ask us to correct your records. If you believe information in your record is incorrect or incomplete, you may ask us in writing to amend it. We may deny the request, and if we do we will tell you why in writing and you may submit a statement of disagreement that will be kept with your record.
  • Get a list of disclosures. You may request an accounting of certain disclosures we have made of your information. For substance use disorder records you may request a list of disclosures made from an electronic record for the three years before your request under 42 C.F.R. § 2.25, and a list of disclosures made by an intermediary for the three years before your request under 42 C.F.R. § 2.24.
  • Ask us to limit what we use or share. You may ask us to restrict the use or disclosure of your information for treatment, payment, or health care operations, including for substance use disorder records under 42 C.F.R. § 2.26. We are not required to agree to every request. We must agree if you ask us not to send information about a service to your health plan and you have paid for that service in full out of pocket, unless the law requires us to share it.
  • Ask us to contact you confidentially. You may ask us to contact you at a different address or phone number, or in a different way. We will accommodate reasonable requests and will not ask you why.
  • Get a copy of this Notice. You may ask for a paper copy at any time, even if you agreed to receive it electronically.
  • Discuss this Notice with us. You may ask the Privacy Officer to explain anything in this Notice.
  • Be told if your information is breached. We will notify you if a breach occurs that compromises the privacy or security of your information.
  • Opt out of fundraising. If we ever contact you for fundraising, you may tell us not to contact you again for that purpose, and we will honor it.
  • Choose someone to act for you. If you have given someone medical power of attorney, or if someone is your legal guardian or your personal representative, that person can exercise these rights for you. We will verify the person’s authority first.

Changes to This Notice

We may change this Notice at any time, and the changed Notice will apply to information we already have about you as well as to information we receive in the future. A material change will not take effect before the effective date of the revised Notice, unless the law requires otherwise. The current Notice is posted in our waiting areas where you can read it without identifying yourself as a person receiving substance use disorder services, is posted on brownbehavioral.com, and is available from the front desk at either office on request. We will give you a copy of the revised Notice at your next visit after a change, and we will give a copy to anyone who asks, whether or not that person is a patient.

How to File a Complaint

If you believe your privacy rights have been violated, you may file a complaint with us, with the federal government, or with both. Filing a complaint will not affect your care in any way, and retaliation against you for filing a complaint is prohibited.

  • With BBHS. Contact our Privacy Officer using the information below. You may complain in person, by telephone, by email, or in writing. We will investigate and respond to you.
  • With the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. Office for Civil Rights, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, 200 Independence Avenue SW, Room 509F, HHH Building, Washington, D.C. 20201. Telephone 1-877-696-6775. Complaints may be filed online at www.hhs.gov/ocr/privacy/hipaa/complaints. For substance use disorder records you may file directly with the Secretary of Health and Human Services.
