Funding

Public investment that expands access to care in rural Tennessee and Mississippi.

Investing in Rural Health

Public Partnerships That Expand Access

Public funding allows Brown Behavioral Health Services to reach more patients, grow our workforce, and bring care directly into rural communities across Tennessee and Mississippi.

Funding Acknowledgment

Programs described on this page are supported in whole or in part by the funding agencies listed. The contents are the responsibility of Brown Behavioral Health Services, PLLC and do not necessarily represent the official views of the funding agencies.

For funding partnerships or program inquiries, contact contact@brownbehavioral.com or call 731-228-9068.

Tennessee
$3,577,860

2025 to 2028

Tennessee Department of Health

Healthcare Rural Resiliency Program

Expands primary care, mental health, and substance use services in Hardeman County, Tennessee, including a mobile medical unit serving communities across the county, school-based telehealth, behavioral health services for Whiteville Correctional Facility, remote patient monitoring, added licensed counselors, and an annual Rural Health Fair each October.

2026 Highlights

  • Hosted the Rural Community Health Fair in Bolivar (July 2026): 578 attendees and 227 children and adolescents screened for depression, anxiety, and substance use
  • Mobile clinic services and screenings across Hardeman County
  • Presented on the HRP panel at the West Tennessee Regional Health Council (August 2026)
  • Trained staff in addiction and psychiatric medication treatment (August 2026)
  • Opened a third location through a University of Memphis partnership (September 2026)
  • Advanced suicide prevention and pediatric care through TSPN and the Cumberland Pediatric Foundation
Tennessee
$1,500,000

2026 to 2030

Tennessee Department of Health

Creating Healthier Rural Communities Through the End Zone for Chronic Disease Prevention: The Blue Plate Project

A community food access and chronic disease prevention initiative in Hardeman County featuring a community garden, a food pantry network serving Bolivar, Hickory Valley, Whiteville, Toone, and Grand Junction, and recurring nutrition and chronic disease education classes.

Mississippi
$448,140

2026 to 2027

Mississippi State Department of Health, Rural Health Transformation Program

Workforce Expansion Initiative: Project MENTOR MS

Builds the rural behavioral health workforce in North Mississippi through preceptor development, clinical rotations for students, apprenticeships, and mobile clinic training sites in Marshall, Tate, Benton, Alcorn, and Tippah counties.

Tennessee
$43,000

2026

Tennessee Department of Health

Perinatal Behavioral Health Workforce Development: BLOOM Perinatal Fellowship

Supports supervision, training, and specialty certification for clinicians serving mothers and families during the perinatal period.

National
National Selection

2026 to 2027

National Rural Health Resource Center, funded by HRSA’s Federal Office of Rural Health Policy

Rural Healthcare Provider Transition Project

BBHS was one of five organizations selected nationally to receive technical assistance that prepares rural providers for value-based care.
